## 1. Simulation Methodology

### Cohort window

Retirement label `W` maps to runtime start `W-1`: the initial withdrawal is
taken at the previous month's closing price. A horizon of `T` months therefore
consumes `T+1` market snapshots and `T` return intervals. The canonical
`1,739`-cohort universe runs from `1871-02` through `2015-12`.

### Ordering and fees

For the workbook-conformant production path the monthly order is: withdraw,
then apply the market return. The production workbook oracle uses the additive
monthly expense ratio:

```text
portfolio_return_t = w_eq * equity_real_t + w_bond * bond_real_t - fee_annual / 12
```

The historical published-ERN comparison used a different convention: T+1 terms
and a multiplicative fee. That comparison is retired and is not an active
validation target; its p49 table and source fixture have been removed. The
distinction remains historical methodology context and must not determine
production expected values.

### Forward projection

Persisted market/CPI data are historical only and end at `2026-06` by default;
projection begins `2026-07`. Runtime coverage is derived from the latest
included cohort plus its maximum horizon, so canonical Part 3 (last cohort
`2015-12`, 60-year horizon) extends through `2075-12` (`2,460` snapshots).
Studies may configure an earlier historical cutoff and their own forward
assumptions. Monthly forward rates use `(1 + annual_rate) ** (1/12) - 1`:

| Asset | Months 1-120 | Month 121 onward |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equity | 4.0% | 5.5% |
| Bonds | 2.5% | 2.25% |

### CAPE conditioning

Four-level regimes: `BELOW_15` (<15), `MODERATE` (15-20), `HIGH` (20-30), and
`EXTREME` (>=30). Part 3 four-regime classification uses the previous month's
Shiller CAPE (`CAPE_{t-1}`), matching the workbook's `CAPE (lag 1M)`
convention. The binary Part 19/20 population also uses `CAPE at t-1 > 20`
(`HIGH`), falling back to the first cohort's own CAPE when no prior observation
exists. A cohort lacking a prior-month observation receives no four-regime
classification and is excluded from four-regime statistics. `cape_available`
and `cape_value` describe cohort-month CAPE and are independent of
`cape_regime`, so `(cape_value=None, cape_regime="EXTREME")` is valid.
`cape_regime_unavailable` counts entries without a usable prior-month CAPE
observation. Part 3's `excluded_no_cape` counts units without four-regime
metadata, not necessarily missing cohort-month CAPE. `require_cape` continues
to govern cohort-month CAPE availability independently. In the canonical
universe the first cohort (`1871-02`) has a prior observation (`1871-01`), so
no cohort is excluded. CAPE is the workbook-only series defined in
`DATASETS.md`.

### Part 52 FFR treatment

The Part 52 loan rate is FFR plus a fixed spread, divided by 12, deflated by
`CPI_{t-1}/CPI_t`, with a one-month FFR lag and interest applied before the
current draw/repayment. The historical FFR segment is workbook-native through
`2021-12`. The frozen FRED extension is the same H.15 effective-rate measure and
is admissible only in the historical diagnostic. Forward-filled months are
never part of historical acceptance. See `DATASETS.md` for provenance and
restrictions.

Anchors are values stated by the published ERN articles. They are independent
of the current implementation and must not be re-baselined when data or code
changes. Differences are findings to classify, not permission to update the
anchor.

#### 2016 historical replication configuration

`src/fbf/core/research/config/part3_2016.yaml` is a separate, non-canonical
configuration for the December 2016 article's Part 3 assumptions. It retains the
canonical `1,739`-cohort universe (`1871-02` through `2015-12`) and the 30-year
and 60-year simulation horizons, but uses a `2016-09-01` historical cutoff.

The forward projection uses `6.6%` real equity throughout, `0%` real bonds for
the first 120 projection months and `2.6%` thereafter, zero inflation, and a
`0.05%` additive annual expense ratio. With the September 2016 cutoff, the
first projection phase covers October 2016 through September 2026; the second
phase begins in October 2026.

Under this configuration, all six published anchors are within the one
percentage-point tolerance:

| Anchor | Simulated | Published |
|---|---:|---:|
| A/30Y/CAPE20-30 | 89.66% (`321/358`) | 89% |
| A/60Y/CAPE20-30 | 72.07% (`258/358`) | 72% |
| B/60Y/CAPE20-30 | 71.23% (`255/358`) | 71% |
| C/60Y/overall | 96.26% (`1674/1739`) | 96% |
| C/60Y/CAPE20-30 | 88.83% (`318/358`) | 88% |
| D/60Y/CAPE20-30 | 96.93% (`347/358`) | 97% |

The D/60Y overall diagnostic is `99.08%` (`1723/1739`) and is not a published
anchor. The prior-month CAPE convention changes regime membership: under the
canonical universe the HIGH denominator is `358`, not `359`, and `BELOW_15`
contains `803` rather than `802` cohorts. Published article percentages are
rounded, so rounded headline agreement does not imply exact count reproduction.

This is a historical replication, not a claim of exact reproduction of the
article methodology. Published anchors are approximate integer or chart-derived
values. Remaining uncertainties are the article's exact cohort eligibility and
historical calculation conventions, including fee ordering, withdrawal timing,
and aggregation; CAPE data vintage; and the origin and applicability of each
published value. The engine's timing convention is effectively one month later
than the literal "previous month's closing price" description and empirically
matches the A/60Y and B anchors better, but it has not been conclusively
established as the article's convention. The canonical 2026 configuration
remains a separate study and is not expected to reproduce the 2016 article
anchors without the article-era assumptions above. Its E2E tests assert
deterministic canonical regression snapshots, not the historical 2016 anchors;
comparisons with the published values are diagnostic only.

The current v2.0 workbook is a methodological reference, not proof that every
detail of the original 2016 GNU Octave research engine has been reproduced.

### Part 42 — One More Year Syndrome

The historical published-table audit is retired. No Part 42 published table is
an acceptance target. Current coverage is structural and oracle-based only.
The article's approximate baseline failsafe (`~3.6%`) and OMY improvement
(`just under 7.8%`) are research references, not hard gates.

The canonical fixed-horizon timeline counts are 361 snapshots for 30Y + 12m
(13 accumulation, 349 retirement, 348 retirement transitions), 601 for 50Y +
12m (13/589/588), and 601 for 50Y + 24m (25/577/576). The 30Y cohort count is
2,099 and the 45-cell grid has 94,455 units. Fixed-real withdrawals use the
original `$2M` base, not the post-OMY portfolio. Social Security is modeled as
a signed external cash-flow schedule: a real `$3,000` monthly inflow starting
in total simulation month 361 and continuing through the original 50-year
horizon end. In retirement-transition space the payment interval is
`[360, 600)` with no OMY, `[348, 588)` with 12-month OMY, and `[336, 576)` with
24-month OMY, preserving the absolute payment dates. A 30-year horizon has no
payment period and is rejected before planning or execution.

## 3. Acceptance Categories

Keep these categories distinct:

1. **Published anchor** — a value stated by the article or workbook.
   Independent acceptance criterion.
2. **Structural invariant** — grid dimensions, cohort counts, classification
   populations, determinism, or provenance. Not a numerical replication claim.
3. **Canonical regression result** — a pinned fixture generated from a defined
   methodology (for example `workbook_oracle_table.csv`). It protects against
   unintended change; it is not an independent oracle.

Do not present a regression fixture as a published anchor. Do not update
expected values automatically when implementation or canonical inputs change.
